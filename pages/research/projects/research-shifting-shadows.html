Shifting Shadows brings together the Corruption Perceptions Index (CPI), recorded protest events and emigration totals. The report describes an initial 50-country dataset, while some later analyses use a final sample of 35 countries. It uses descriptive statistics, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, ANOVA and regression in RStudio.

Three measures, different scales

The variables are country-level aggregates, not measures of an individual’s views or migration decision. The project compares values and fits exploratory models to look for associations.

Perceptions index

A public-sector corruption perceptions measure; higher scores generally indicate a cleaner public sector.

What the reported models say

The presentation reports a weak CPI-only relationship with emigration (R² around 0.10; p above .05), and a stronger simple association between protest counts and emigration totals (R² around 0.35). An ANOVA comparing protest means by subregion reports p around 0.33, so it does not find a statistically significant difference under that test.

A later multiple regression reports a higher overall fit, but the report and slides shift sample definitions and describe a mixture of predictors. The coefficient interpretation should be checked against the underlying data and script before being treated as a settled result.

Why the association is not a causal pathway

The data are observational and aggregated by country. A relationship between protest counts and emigrant totals cannot tell whether protest activity caused migration. Population size, conflict, labour markets, geography and other unmeasured differences could affect both.

The source slide uses CPI as a perception index, protest events from 2022, and emigration totals from 2020. Different years and raw totals reduce direct comparability.

Read this as an exploratory course project

The sample changes from 50 countries to 35 in parts of the report; not all observations appear to support every model. Counts are not adjusted for population, and the project’s own figures and interpretation merit a consistency check. The embedded deck is kept intact; the notes here make the sample and causal limits visible.
