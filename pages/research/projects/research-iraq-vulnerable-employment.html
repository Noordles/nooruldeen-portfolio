Vulnerable work has declined over the full series. The gap by sex remains.

35 ANNUAL OBSERVATIONS · 1991–2025

The World Bank’s 2025 ILO modelled estimate places vulnerable employment at 36.1% of employed women and 26.0% of employed men, a difference of about 10 percentage points. The total rate falls from 35.4% in 1991 to 27.1% in 2025. These are descriptive estimates, not evidence that any one policy or event caused the change.

What does “vulnerable employment” count?

The indicator is the share of employed people who are either own-account workers without employees or contributing family workers. Those two groups are often less likely to have formal work arrangements or social protection.

It can help describe employment status. It does not count every informal job and it is not an estimate of the full size of Iraq’s shadow economy.

How to read the trend

The total estimate falls by about 8.2 percentage points between 1991 and 2025. The female estimate falls by about 12.1 points and the male estimate by about 7.8 points over the same endpoints.

The annual series moves unevenly. A change in the modelled estimate is a signal to investigate further, not a causal explanation by itself.

Important limits

These are ILO modelled estimates, which may combine reported observations with imputed values and projections. World Bank metadata cautions that imputed estimates carry high uncertainty and should not be used for country rankings. Household survey microdata would be needed to study earnings, household hardship, and lived experience directly.
