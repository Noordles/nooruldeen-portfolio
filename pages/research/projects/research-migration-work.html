PLANNED STUDY

Migration is a household decision made under constraints.

PROJECT PLAN · DATA AND RESULTS NOT YET ADDED

This brief sketches a way to study whether work opportunities and employment security are associated with migration intentions or moves. The association may differ by age, education, gender, family responsibilities, and destination options.

01 / QUESTION

How do work options shape mobility?

Ask how employment availability, earnings stability, and expectations for the future relate to migration intentions and decisions.

02 / EVIDENCE

Join context with lived experience

Potential inputs include comparable labour indicators, migration surveys, and interviews or household survey microdata where access and coverage allow.

03 / METHOD

Separate intention from movement

Define the outcome carefully, account for household and demographic factors, and report where data cannot support causal claims.

Next steps

  • Choose the population and migration outcome.
  • Check survey coverage, consent, and comparability.
  • Map employment measures to the migration question.
  • Publish a transparent descriptive analysis before testing explanations.

Evidence to review

ILO labour data can describe broad employment patterns. Migration decisions require dedicated survey or administrative evidence; labour indicators alone cannot reveal why a person or household moved.
