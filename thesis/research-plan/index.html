My thesis: the question, the system and the evidence

I grew up in Iraq, and I want to understand how its resource wealth relates to services, opportunities and pressure on public finances. For my Economic Informatics thesis at ASE, I am building a Python system that makes the evidence easier to inspect, compare and reproduce.

Research plan reviewed on 16 November 2026. The latest verified application release is 0.2.17, checked on 11 November. Economic observations keep their original dates and source editions.

My approved title is Iraq’s Economic Outlook: Building an Economic Analysis System with Iraq as a Case Study. Professor Cristian Răzvan Uscatu has agreed to coordinate the thesis, and Professor Adriana Ana Maria Davidescu has agreed to work with me. The title is approved; detailed methods and the final case selection remain for discussion. My intended defence is July 2027.

The question and the contribution

How has Iraq’s resource wealth translated into services, productive activity and opportunities, and what evidence could explain the gaps?

This is my working research direction. The approved thesis title remains unchanged; the detailed scope and methods remain open to my professors’ guidance.

I am building a way to investigate Iraq’s economy that keeps the question, source, calculation and limits together. The application is my Economic Informatics contribution; the economic studies show how it works and where its answers stop.

The software question is how to preserve the meaning, source, date, calculation, uncertainty and assumptions needed to investigate an economic question. I want another reader to be able to reconstruct an answer, including a decision that the evidence is insufficient.

The application I have built

The hosted app now brings together the national overview, indicators, country comparisons, government accounts, monthly forecasts, growth models, IMF scenarios, informal employment, and the research library.

Search connects pages, questions, findings, methods and sources. Shared filters, responsive charts, source receipts and analysis downloads help me follow a result back to its inputs. The brand returns to Overview.

The country-comparison page has separate workspaces for Gulf outcomes, development paths, peer similarity, spatial studies and resource rents per resident. The regional and worldwide maps have distinct scopes and test rules. Resource rent per resident uses matching GDP, rent shares and resident population; it is not household income or treasury cash.

The explanations use declared rules and selected data, without calling a generative AI service. The current reader design presents the finding first and keeps methods, original evidence and code available for inspection. HTML, JSON and CSV exports retain the context appropriate to each analysis.

Data windows and source meaning

The longer development history starts in 2003. Annual GDP stays annual, oil rents keep their earlier publication window, and missing observations stay missing. The accepted monthly fiscal experiment still has 126 consecutive records from January 2016 to June 2026. New research collections do not silently extend that training sample.

CBI cumulative within-year accounts are differenced into monthly flows and reconciled back to their source totals. Accounting revisions and flagged values remain visible. Full-year shares require twelve compatible months. The Ministry of Finance’s January–September comparison is a separate reviewed episode, not an updated monthly model input.

Matched Gulf comparisons use Iraq and five peers: Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates. Each case requires a common observed year across its countries and indicators. A missing common year produces a coverage explanation rather than a substituted value or a ranking.

Selected findings and their limits

Oil and mineral receipts dominate recorded revenue

In the preserved 2025 accounts, oil and mineral receipts made up 87.30% of recorded government revenue.

This is a share of recorded receipts, not oil rents or a measure of theft. Annual answers require twelve compatible months and are withheld for missing or flagged inputs. Other receipts are not automatically tax revenue.

CBI bulletin · PDF pp. 67, 69 ↗

Electricity access does not settle reliability

All six economies report 100% population access in 2024. Iraq’s transmission and distribution losses are 58.70% of electricity output, against a five-peer median of 5.69%.

Access, losses and hours of dependable supply are different measurements. There is no common survey year for a six-country firm-outage ranking, so that comparison remains unanswered. These figures do not identify a policy effect.

Health financing and opportunities show further gaps

Public health spending per resident at PPP was 387.83 in Iraq versus a peer median of 2,408.11 in 2023. In 2025, modelled employment-to-population was 35.10% versus 71.77%, and women’s labour-force participation was 10.94% versus 47.20%.

The rows have different years and denominators. Spending is not clinical quality, and employment gaps do not establish their causes. These are separate comparisons, not a combined welfare score.

Flaring volume and intensity tell different stories

Within the World Bank’s June 2026 revised edition, Iraq’s estimated flared-gas volume rose 14.63% from 2012 to 2025 while flaring intensity fell 22.07%. The app also shows Kazakhstan for a bounded comparison.

A lower intensity does not mean a lower total volume. Satellite-derived flared gas is not automatically recoverable gas, electricity output or budget savings; engineering, costs and delivery constraints are still needed.

Real output alongside the GDP share

The latest app adds real manufacturing value added in constant 2015 US dollars alongside manufacturing’s share of GDP, for Iraq and the same five Gulf peers from 2010 to 2024.

A share can change because manufacturing or the GDP denominator changes. Both manufacturing series include refining; neither measures all non-oil activity or proves a diversification policy worked.

The reference test changes the conclusion

For oil rents as a share of GDP, the same world statistic gives p = 0.0020 under unrestricted shuffling and p = 0.0646 when shuffling stays within World Bank reporting regions. The second result is inconclusive at 5%.

Spatial association does not establish spillovers or a causal resource curse. The within-region test does not isolate regional oil prices. Changing the map’s highlight threshold changes the display, not the test sample.

A later fiscal warning, kept separate

A separate review of the Ministry of Finance’s January–September reports finds that the recorded shortfall widened from 8.13 trillion IQD in 2025 to 36.26 trillion in 2026. Revenue fell 42.5% and spending fell 10.9%.

This reviewed episode has not extended the app’s accepted CBI forecast sample beyond June 2026. Nine-month accounts are not full-year outcomes or IMF projections. A monthly extension needs overlapping-source reconciliation and successive cumulative totals.

Forecasts, growth models and conditional scenarios

The initial revenue experiment compares a last-value benchmark, a same-month-last-year benchmark, AR(1), ARIMA and seasonal ARIMA on the same 24 one-step targets, July 2024 to June 2026, starting with 102 training months. Each fit uses earlier observations only. AR(1) has the lowest MAE and ARIMA the lowest RMSE in that saved experiment. This is a historical test on revised source tables, not a real-time vintage reconstruction or a fresh forecast.

One-, three- and six-month evaluation is available separately. The annual multivariate growth experiment did not beat the expanding-mean benchmark across eight common test years. I keep that negative result visible. The reviewed VECM candidate is not accepted: the annual sample is short and quarterly revisions, mappings and valuation differences remain unresolved.

April and October 2026 IMF outlooks remain distinct source releases. The scenario calculator keeps the April annual baseline and changes explicit assumptions about prices, export quantities, other revenue and spending. It holds the stated remaining quantities fixed. It is accounting sensitivity, not a probability, a debt-dynamics model or a causal reform estimate.

How I will evaluate the system

My evaluation uses published evidence, independent calculations, source checks, forecast benchmarks and reproducible exports. It does not claim that a user study proved better understanding. My first-submission packet was prepared and checked; that does not establish submission or approval.

The evidence checks cover input integrity, numerical validity, correct definitions, method assumptions, evidence status, reproducibility, actual controls and limited reuse on compatible countries. A successful test verifies its stated case; it does not prove that every original publication is accurate or that the software understands arbitrary data.

At the defence I can reproduce the 2025 oil-and-mineral share from the exported original cumulative rows and monthly flows, then show why an incomplete year is withheld. A second case reconstructs real-manufacturing growth and GDP-share changes from the exported matched endpoints. Both retain the source edition, calculation and limits.

The reviewed release exercised all nine app pages at desktop, tablet and phone widths, including search, navigation, comparison workspaces and downloads. These checks establish operation in the checked conditions. A controlled before-and-after performance comparison and a manual screen-reader review remain to be completed.

Writing and scope

The supplied supervisor guidance organizes the thesis into an introduction, a focused review of existing applications and requirements, a main design-and-implementation chapter, and conclusions with realistic extensions. Chapter 1 should establish the need for a better system without treating my own app as an existing competitor. The main contribution explains requirements, justified technologies, data design, algorithms, interface, testing and maintenance.

My first packet was prepared and checked on 9 November. I do not infer submission, acceptance or approval of every method from that preparation. I will keep the writing focused on demonstrable cases, while the research library can retain wider questions and reading.

Questions I want to develop next

Gas, electricity and service delivery

Connect plant fuel, gas quality, costs, generation, grid losses and actual supply hours. Flaring estimates alone cannot tell me how much electricity could be delivered or at what cost.

Diversification beyond a changing GDP share

Extend the real-manufacturing case with comparable industry and export data. UNCTAD product concentration is a candidate, with six-country coverage still unverified. Fuel-export shares measure fuel exposure, not the same thing.

Institutions, conflict and project delivery

Compare mechanisms using published studies, dated project records and service outcomes. Governance indicators need their uncertainty and revision vintage preserved. A residual in an account or a perception score is not evidence that a named person stole money.

A focused thesis and an honest evaluation

Keep the negative growth-model result, investigate forecast diagnostics and document failed or blocked cases. The VECM remains unaccepted until compatible data and a defensible specification exist. A controlled performance comparison and manual screen-reader review remain open.

Private correspondence, drafts, source archives and the wider unreviewed question registry are outside the public website export.

Where I hope to take it

Eventually, I want this program to be useful for any country, with a clear way to import different datasets. For now, I am building and testing it around the Iraqi data I understand and care about. Once that works well, I want to make the import process, country choices and analysis easier to adapt. Different datasets will still need their own checks and suitable methods; that wider version is something I want to develop after the core thesis.
