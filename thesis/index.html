I want to understand

What interests me is the space between having valuable resources and turning them into lasting development. I want to look at the evidence carefully, ask better questions, and build a tool that makes the numbers easier to explore.

01

How has Iraq developed since 2003?

I want to compare growth, real income per person and manufacturing, then understand the context behind the patterns.

02

What puts pressure on the budget?

I am examining recorded revenue and spending month by month, while keeping oil-related income and accounting adjustments visible.

03

Can a forecast improve on a simple baseline?

I compare five methods on the same months and inspect their errors and uncertainty before choosing a final model.

04

How much do the assumptions change the outlook?

I can change an oil-price or spending assumption and calculate the resulting pressure around a published annual baseline.

I want to understand what the evidence shows. A country comparison can help me spot a pattern, but it cannot prove what caused it.

Does a more complex model help?

I test five methods on the same 24 months, July 2024–June 2026. Each predicts one month ahead. MAE is the average size of the errors; RMSE gives bigger mistakes more weight. Both are in trillion Iraqi dinars.

WHAT I FOUND SO FAR AR(1) has the lowest MAE. ARIMA(1,1,1) has the lowest RMSE. I still need to check the model assumptions and remaining patterns in the errors before making my final choice.

These saved predictions use data ending in June 2026 to look at July–September. They are not a fresh forecast made today. The shaded 95% band shows uncertainty under the model's assumptions. So far, I have tested accuracy one month ahead.

What if oil prices fall?

I use the IMF's April 2026 yearly projections as a starting point. Change the assumptions to see how the budget balance changes.

I keep spending, export quantities, other revenue, GDP and the exchange rate fixed here. I choose the share of revenue that responds to oil prices. This shows what happens under those assumptions; it does not say how likely the scenario is.

What makes a country comparable?

I compare countries using their 2003–2007 oil rents, real GDP per person and manufacturing. Change how much each feature matters to see how the ranking changes. My calculation uses log GDP per person to reduce the effect of very large income differences.

A lower distance means a closer match on these features, among the countries I have loaded. This does not prove why their economies developed differently. I may add Bahrain after checking its data.

I use Python to check the inputs, prepare the observations and run the experiments. The interface helps me inspect the results and export their evidence.
